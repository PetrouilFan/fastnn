# W8A8 and AVX-VNNI: policy and measured baseline

This note records what W8A8 costs and earns on the two machines the project
benchmarks on, and the rule a W8A8 planner must follow so that enabling it on a
VNNI host does not change behaviour on an AVX2-only host.

Nothing here is implemented yet: `prepare --precision w8a8` still refuses, and
`docs/v3/status.md` tracks the executor work under #103. This file is the
measurement and policy reference the executor must satisfy.

## Hardware

| host | ISA | VNNI |
|---|---|---|
| Intel Core Ultra 7 155H | AVX2, AVX-VNNI | yes (CPUID leaf 7.1 EAX bit 4) |
| AMD Ryzen 7 3700X | AVX2 | no |
| AVX-512 on either host | absent | - |

oneDNN dispatches on CPUID at runtime, so one binary serves both.

## Full-model baseline (ONNX Runtime, YOLO11n 640)

Static QDQ: `QUInt8` activations, `QInt8` per-channel weights. 3 warm-ups + 30
forwards, median, one process per configuration. Calibration was synthetic, so
this is a latency and RSS baseline, not an accuracy gate.

| threads | f32 | W8A8 | speedup | f32 peak | W8A8 peak |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 73.02 ms | 37.38 ms | 1.95x | 183.0 MiB | 120.9 MiB |
| 4 | 28.09 ms | 16.27 ms | 1.73x | 183.4 MiB | 121.9 MiB |
| 8 | 21.38 ms | 13.97 ms | 1.53x | 184.3 MiB | 122.5 MiB |
| 11 | 22.14 ms | 15.00 ms | 1.48x | 185.1 MiB | 122.7 MiB |

## oneDNN int8 vs f32, with and without VNNI

`u8 x s8 -> u8`, per-tensor scales, fused ReLU. `DNNL_MAX_CPU_ISA=AVX2` removes
the VNNI kernels. Median of three alternating runs of 100 forwards.

| case | f32 | int8 VNNI | int8 AVX2 | int8 vs f32 | VNNI gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1x1 64->64 80x80 | 0.49 | 0.18 | 0.285 | 2.7x | 1.57x |
| 3x3 64->64 80x80 | 3.90 | 1.44 | 2.00 | 2.7x | 1.39x |
| 3x3 128->128 40x40 | 3.85 | 1.15 | 2.51 | 3.3x | 2.18x |
| 1x1 256->128 40x40 | 0.88 | 0.49 | 0.61 | 1.8x | 1.24x |
| 3x3 32->64 160x160 | 7.90 | 3.94 | 4.10 | 2.0x | 1.04x |
| depthwise 3x3 64 80x80 | 0.15 | 0.62 | 0.29 | 0.23x | 0.47x |

VNNI is worth 1.2-2.2x on dense convolutions. Depthwise int8 is 4.3x slower
than f32 and must stay f32. Per-channel scales are speed-neutral relative to
per-tensor (the earlier 2x reading did not reproduce); use per-channel for
accuracy.

## Layout is the gate, not the ISA

- NHWC and oneDNN-chosen blocked layouts are equal for int8.
- `nChw16c` int8 took **22 s per forward**; plain NCHW falls to the reference
  kernel (2.4-7.1 s).
- f32 NHWC is within noise of f32 blocked and 2-70x faster than f32 NCHW, so one
  NHWC family can serve both precisions.

The old "848x slower without VNNI" figure came from measuring the NCHW
reference path. The corrected measurement is 0.97-1.20x f32 on AVX2, and that is
what `api::refuse_unexecutable_precision` now reports.

## Rule for a VNNI plan that is safe on AVX2

1. **One binary, runtime dispatch.** No `target-cpu=native`, no crate-level
   `target_feature`; custom SIMD kernels pick AVX2 or scalar from CPUID.
2. **Key plans by ISA.** A plan prepared on a VNNI host encodes packed weights
   and block sizes that AVX2 cannot use. `load` must select an ISA-matched plan
   or build a portable f32 fallback; the artifact carries at least one f32 plan.
3. **Decide by measurement, not by a feature bit.** At prepare time, measure the
   candidate int8 segment against f32 (conv + epilogue + requantization) and
   enable int8 only above a threshold that covers the extra passes (start at
   1.15x). On the 3700X that leaves dense convolutions at f32 parity; on the
   155H it selects int8 for 1x1/3x3 and leaves depthwise f32.
4. **NHWC for int8.** Never emit NCHW or `nChw16c` for an int8 convolution.
5. **Do not fuse SiLU into int8.** oneDNN's int8 swish is 3.6-12x slower than
   int8 ReLU. Use a linear `u8` convolution followed by a vectorized
   `u8 -> SiLU -> u8` pass.
6. **Pools need a JIT-eligible layout.** On AVX2, oneDNN's JIT pooling rejects
   plain NCHW (`ncsp_fmt_tag` is only set for AVX-512), so a channels-last or
   blocked descriptor is required; otherwise the scalar kernel must run.

## Before the executor: measured tail costs

YOLO11n 640, 8 threads, fused plan: `conv+swish` is 72.8 % and `max_pool` 7.6 %.
Unfused: conv 67 %, sigmoid+mul 18.4 %. The scalar SiLU over the model's 16.8 M
elements costs 46.9 ms at one thread; a specialized NHWC max-pool over the same
windows is 0.10 ms against 9.8 ms for the per-output coordinate decode.

Those two kernels are worth fixing before W8A8: they shrink the surface the
quantized path has to beat and they benefit both hosts.
